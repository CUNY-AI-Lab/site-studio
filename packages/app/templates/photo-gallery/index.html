Brooklyn Voices

A photo essay exploring community and identity in changing neighborhoods

Photography by Alex Kim | Spring 2024

Over six months, I documented the lives and stories of longtime residents in Brooklyn's rapidly changing neighborhoods. This project captures moments of resilience, community, and the complex emotions of watching your neighborhood transform around you.

Mrs. Rodriguez stands in front of her bodega on Knickerbocker Avenue, where she's worked for 35 years. The storefront has become a community gathering place.

Sunday morning at the community garden, where neighbors of all ages come together to tend plots passed down through generations.

Marcus, a barber at Flatbush Cuts for 28 years, gives a trim while sharing stories about the neighborhood's past.

The annual block party on Jefferson Street brings together old-timers and newcomers. For one day, the street transforms into a celebration of community.

Children play in the shadow of new luxury condos rising behind their playground.

Ahmed at his halal cart, a fixture at the corner of Bedford and Nassau for 15 years.

Evening light filters through the windows of a rent-stabilized building, home to families who've lived here for decades.

The Martinez family gathers for Sunday dinner in their apartment. "We won't leave," grandmother Carmen says. "This is our home."

Old timers play dominoes in McCarren Park, a tradition that continues despite the park's transformation.

A mural on Bushwick Avenue commemorates neighborhood history and residents who've passed, painted by local youth.

As the sun sets over Brooklyn, residents gather on stoops and fire escapes, continuing traditions of community and connection that define these neighborhoods.

Artist's Note

This project began as an exploration of gentrification but became something more personal— a documentation of the human connections that persist even as neighborhoods change. The people I photographed shared their stories with remarkable openness, speaking about loss and adaptation, but also about resilience and hope.

My goal was not to romanticize the past or demonize change, but to honor the lived experiences of longtime residents whose stories are often overlooked in discussions of urban development. These photographs are a testament to their presence, their persistence, and their right to remain in the communities they helped build.

— Alex Kim, May 2024

About the Photographer

Alex Kim is a documentary photographer and graduate student at Hunter College, CUNY. Their work focuses on urban communities, migration, and social change. Previous projects have been exhibited at the Bronx Documentary Center and published in The New York Times and Curbed.
