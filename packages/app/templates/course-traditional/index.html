Course Description

This course examines major themes in American history from 1945 to the present, including the Cold War, civil rights movements, political realignments, economic transformations, and cultural change. We will analyze primary sources, engage with historiographical debates, and consider how the past shapes our present.

Learning Objectives

  • Understand major political, social, and economic developments in postwar America
  • Analyze primary source documents and historical arguments critically
  • Develop research and writing skills appropriate to historical scholarship
  • Connect historical events to contemporary issues and debates

Required Texts

  • Patterson, James T. Grand Expectations: The United States, 1945-1974
  • Cowie, Jefferson. Stayin' Alive: The 1970s and the Last Days of the Working Class
  • Additional readings available on Blackboard

Assignments

Primary Source Analysis Papers (3 papers, 4-5 pages each)

Analyze assigned primary source documents using historical context and critical reading skills. Papers should identify the source's argument, historical context, and significance. Use Chicago-style citations.

Due dates: September 21, October 26, November 14

Midterm Exam

In-class essay exam covering material from Weeks 1-5. You will answer two essay questions drawing on lectures, readings, and class discussions.

Date: October 12

Final Research Paper (12-15 pages)

Original research paper on a topic related to post-1945 American history. Must use at least 8 scholarly sources including primary materials. Proposal due October 31.

Due date: December 8, 5 PM (submit via Blackboard)
